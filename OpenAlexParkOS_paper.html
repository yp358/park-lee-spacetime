OpenAlexParkOS: A Four-Tier Ontological Architecture
for Sovereign-Directed, AI-Assisted Operating-System Pedagogy

The Park–Lee Spacetime Model of CreOS, EthOS, LogOS, and PathOS in an 8-bit Teaching Environment

David Ye Joon Park1

1OpenAlexParkOS Project (Independent Research)

September 2026

Abstract

We present OpenAlexParkOS, an abridged operating-systems curriculum and reference implementation designed to introduce core OS concepts — memory, processes, scheduling, and I/O — to early-childhood learners (nominally age five) and their families through an 8-bit teaching machine. The system is organized around a four-tier ontological architecture we call the Park–Lee Spacetime model, comprising creos/ (a human-only declarative root), ethos/ (a human-authored, AI-readable ethical framework), logos/ (a human-authored, AI-readable logical and architectural specification), and pathos/ (an AI-directed generative domain). Each tier fixes a distinct scope of agency and a distinct permission level — forbidden, read-only, or directed-generative — that an AI collaborator may hold with respect to it, so that architectural governance is enforced by directory-level structure rather than by convention alone. We map this structure onto a pedagogical relationship in which the adult family member acts as Sovereign (the human controller who instantiates creos/, ethos/, and logos/) and the child acts as an Independent Subject who explores, plays in, and gradually authors within the AI-directed pathos/ layer. We describe the 8-bit machine model chosen for tractability and visual legibility, the resulting directory and permission scheme, and the design rationale connecting each tier to a corresponding concern in conventional operating-systems theory (policy, mechanism, and generative surface). We conclude with a discussion of the model's limitations and directions for empirical evaluation with child learners.

Keywords: operating systems education, human-in-the-loop AI governance, capability-based access control, child-directed computing, 8-bit architecture, AI-assisted curriculum design.

1. Introduction

Operating-systems instruction is traditionally deferred to undergraduate computer science, on the premise that concepts such as address spaces, scheduling, and privilege separation require substantial mathematical and programming maturity. OpenAlexParkOS is a case study in relaxing that premise: we ask what remains true and teachable about an operating system when its word size is reduced to 8 bits, its address space to a few hundred cells, and its explanation to the vocabulary of a five-year-old, while an AI collaborator handles the generative and explanatory load under fixed human-authored constraints.

The central engineering problem this raises is not instructional but architectural: how does a household deploy an AI system into a child's learning environment while keeping the values, rules, and safety boundaries of that environment strictly outside the AI's authority to alter? Our answer is to make that boundary a property of the file system and the permission model, not merely a prompt or a policy document. We call the resulting four-tier structure the Park–Lee Spacetime architecture, and we use it as the organizing principle for both the software repository and the pedagogical relationship it encodes.

2. Related Work

Our approach draws on three separate lineages. First, constructionist computing education — Papert's LOGO environment and its description in Mindstorms [1], and its modern descendants such as Scratch and ScratchJr [2] — established that young children can build genuine mental models of computational processes when given a sufficiently concrete, manipulable representation. Second, classical operating-systems theory [3, 4] separates mechanism (how something is done) from policy (what should be done), and separately, capability-based and ring-structured privilege models restrict which subjects may modify which resources. Third, current work on human oversight of AI systems argues for constraining an AI's write access to a bounded, auditable surface while keeping foundational specifications under exclusively human control. The Park–Lee Spacetime architecture is, in effect, an application of the second and third lineages to the content and delivery mechanism of the first: a child's OS curriculum, generated and narrated by an AI that cannot touch the curriculum's declared values or rules.

3. The Park–Lee Spacetime Architecture

The architecture is a strict derivation chain of four domains. Each domain is instantiated by the one before it, and agency narrows — from purely human, to human-authored-but-AI-legible, to AI-directed — as the chain proceeds:

Human() → creos/ → ethos/ → logos/ → pathos/

3.1 CreOS — the Declarative Root

creos/ holds the axioms of the system: the small set of non-negotiable statements that define what the household's OpenAlexParkOS is for and whom it serves. It is written and revised solely by the human Sovereign and is forbidden to the AI collaborator — not merely unwritable in practice, but outside the AI's scope of consideration entirely, in the way firmware is outside the scope of an application. Every other tier exists to instantiate, elaborate, or express something already settled here.

3.2 EthOS — Ethical Instantiation

ethos/ takes the axioms of creos/ and expresses them as operating values and boundaries: what the system should protect, what it should never do, and how it should treat the child as a learner. It remains human-authored, but unlike creos/ it is read-only to the AI — the AI may consult it as a constant frame of reference when generating content in pathos/, but cannot edit it.

3.3 LogOS — Logical Architecture

logos/ renders ethos/ into operational form: protocols, schemas, and the rules that govern how the 8-bit machine behaves, how lessons are sequenced, and how the AI is permitted to respond. Like ethos/, it is human-authored and read-only to the AI. Where ethos/ supplies the "why," logos/ supplies the checkable "how" — the closest analogue, in conventional OS terms, to a kernel's policy and interface contracts.

3.4 PathOS — the Generative, AI-Directed Domain

pathos/ is the one tier in which the AI has generative authority: lesson narration, procedurally varied practice problems, visual and sound output from the 8-bit machine, and adaptive pacing. This authority is bounded on every side by the tiers above it — the AI can create freely inside the space those tiers define, but cannot widen that space itself. This is also the tier the child, as Independent Subject, experiences directly.

4. Design Philosophy and Motivation

The derivation chain is deliberately one-directional and asymmetric: nothing downstream can rewrite something upstream. The project's stated design philosophy draws this pattern from the vine-and-branches image in John 15:5 — "I am the vine, you are the branches" — in which fruit-bearing (here, the generative work of pathos/) is possible only through an unbroken connection back to the root, and cannot substitute for or redefine the root itself. Practically, this motivates a hard architectural rule rather than a soft one: governance is enforced by what the AI is structurally permitted to read and write, not only by what it is instructed to do.

5. The 8-bit Teaching Machine

OpenAlexParkOS targets a minimal virtual machine with an 8-bit word, a small (single-page, 256-cell) address space, an 8-entry instruction set, and a handful of memory-mapped registers driving a simple visual/audio display. Constraining the machine to 8 bits serves three pedagogical goals:

(a) Countability. A five-year-old can count to 255, or at least accept it as "a full box of blocks," giving memory a concrete size rather than an abstract one.
(b) Visibility. Every register and memory cell can be rendered on-screen simultaneously as a small grid of blocks or lights, so state changes are watched rather than imagined.
(c) Direct correspondence. Each of the machine's few instructions maps to a single physical action in a companion unplugged activity (e.g., a physical box labeled with the byte, a card with the opcode), letting the family enact a "process" or an "interrupt" by hand before seeing it on screen.

The machine's specification, instruction set, and memory map are authored in logos/ and are therefore fixed with respect to the AI: the AI may narrate, animate, and vary exercises that use the machine, but may not change what the machine is.

6. The Sovereign / Independent-Subject Pedagogical Model

We map the architecture directly onto the family's roles. The adult (or adults) instantiate creos/, ethos/, and logos/ for their household — deciding, once, what the system is for and what it will never do — and act as Sovereign over the whole system thereafter. The child is the Independent Subject: free to explore, make mistakes, ask questions, and direct their own play inside pathos/, without that freedom ever extending to the layers that define the system's values or rules. This gives the child real agency — the AI genuinely follows the child's curiosity within the sandbox — while keeping the boundary of that sandbox a matter of settled parental authorship rather than of the AI's momentary judgment.

7. Discussion and Limitations

The architecture's central claim is structural, not pedagogical: it does not by itself guarantee that a five-year-old will learn OS concepts, only that whatever the AI generates while teaching them stays within human-authored bounds. Evaluating the pedagogical claim requires longitudinal study with child participants and is outside the scope of this paper. We also note that the 8-bit machine is a teaching model, not a production OS, and that "read-only" and "forbidden" are currently enforced by file-system permissions and prompt-level instruction rather than by a formally verified reference monitor; hardening this boundary with OS-level capability enforcement is future work.

8. Conclusion

OpenAlexParkOS proposes that an AI-assisted, child-directed operating-systems curriculum can be made both concrete (via an 8-bit teaching machine) and safely governed (via the Park–Lee Spacetime's four-tier permission model) at the same time. By fixing agency to directory structure — forbidden, read-only, or directed-generative — the architecture keeps the household's declared values and rules outside the AI's reach while still giving the AI genuine creative latitude to teach within them.
